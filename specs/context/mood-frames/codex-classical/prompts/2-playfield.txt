Art brief:
Product: Piano Arcade, a Japanese-style arcade rhythm game played on a real 88-key digital piano in Chrome: notes fall toward the keys, every note is judged (PERFECT, GREAT, GOOD, MISS), and a learning side shows you what to practice.

Art direction, "classical paper": the whole interface is printed matter. It feels like a fine engraved music edition (Henle, Bärenreiter) and a letterpress concert program, but it is still clearly a game: bold, legible at a glance, with a few confident celebratory moments. Restraint over decoration: one ornament per panel at most, lots of calm cream space.

Rendering: a high-fidelity, flat, straight-on UI design mockup of one screen, as a 1920x1080 browser screenshot. No browser chrome, no device frame, no desk, no hands, no people, no perspective tilt. Crisp edges, precise alignment, a clear hierarchy. Text is minimal, set large, and spelled exactly as given; leave out any text not listed.

Palette (use only these): paper cream #f4ecdc with a faint laid-paper texture (never pure white); ink #1b1712 for text and staff lines; gold leaf #b8892b, slightly metallic, for the most important accent; viridian #1f6f5c for the right hand; Prussian blue #2b4c7e for the left hand; vermilion #c0392b only for a WRONG note; faded rose-grey #a88a8f for MISS.

Type: titles in a high-contrast engraving serif like Didot or Bodoni; labels in small caps with wide letter-spacing; big numbers in elegant lining serif numerals.

Components: primary buttons are ink-filled with gold-leaf small-caps text and a hairline gold inner border, gently letterpress-embossed; secondary buttons are cream with a hairline ink border. Cards are cream paper panels with a thin double rule and a whisper of shadow.

Avoid: parchment that looks old, stained or burned; heavy filigree; fantasy or medieval styling; neon; glow effects; pixel art; random musical-note confetti; lens flare.

Screen: gameplay, the moment of playing. This is the hardest screen: it must stay perfectly readable while notes fall.

Layout: the bottom 18% is a full 88-key piano keyboard seen straight-on from above, ivory white keys and ebony black keys, rendered cleanly. Two keys are pressed: one tinted viridian, one tinted Prussian blue. Directly above the keys runs a thin gold-leaf hit line across the full width.

Above it, the highway on cream paper: 88 very faint vertical pencil-grey lanes, each aligned exactly over its key. Rounded note bars fall down the lanes like strokes of watercolor ink: right-hand notes viridian (#1f6f5c), left-hand notes Prussian blue (#2b4c7e). Notes far from the line are pale and translucent, near notes are saturated, and the two notes touching the hit line have a small crisp gold burst. About fifteen notes on screen in total, in two clusters, one per hand. The lanes stay calm and empty of decoration.

Edges only, never over the lanes: top left, the title "Minuet in G" in serif with, beside it, a slim one-line strip of engraved score showing the current bar with a small gold caret over the current note. Top right, a cream card holding a huge serif numeral "87" with the small-caps label "COMBO", the score "412,550", and a small gold wax-seal badge "×4". Near the right end of the hit line, a small judgment in gold small caps "PERFECT" with "−8 ms" beneath it in tiny ink.

Mood: a page of music come alive. Calm center, elegant edges.
