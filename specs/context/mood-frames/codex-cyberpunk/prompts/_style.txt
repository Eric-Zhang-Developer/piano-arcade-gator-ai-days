Product: Piano Arcade, a game that teaches you piano. You play a real 88-key digital piano in Chrome; notes fall toward the keys, every note is judged to the millisecond (PERFECT, GREAT, GOOD, MISS), and a tutor reads each take and tells you exactly what to practice next. The brand feeling in one line: "a machine that hears every note, and a reason to play one more time." Precision you can trust, plus the thrill of an arcade.

Art direction, "tasteful cyberpunk": an instrument-grade HUD, like Formula 1 telemetry, a fighter-jet heads-up display or Valorant's interface, applied to a piano. Mostly empty dark space. Thin 1px hairline frames, small corner brackets, tick marks and calibration marks; small monospace labels in caps for data; big confident condensed sans-serif numerals for scores and combo. Calm, precise surfaces, with color and glow saved for the few moments that matter (a hit spark, the combo, the rank). Arcade heritage shows in the big numbers, the rank stamp and the energy, never in clutter. It must look like a polished, shipped product made by a team with real taste.

Rendering: a high-fidelity, flat, straight-on UI design mockup of one screen, as a 1920x1080 browser screenshot. No browser chrome, no device frame, no desk, no people, no hands, no perspective tilt. Crisp edges, precise alignment, strong hierarchy. Text is minimal, large enough to read, and spelled exactly as given; leave out any text not listed.

Avoid: neon soup (everything glowing), a synthwave sun, a perspective grid floor, chrome 80s lettering, rain-soaked city skylines, glitch effects everywhere, random katakana, pixel art, lens flares, stock "AI art" particles.
