## Report

**Layout.** In perspective. The highway fills the top ~55% of the screen. Lane guides are tall arches that bend toward a horizon line near the top. A thick silver judgment bar runs full width at ~55% height. Under it is an on-screen strip of ~28 equal pale-teal "keys" with no black keys drawn. Combo sits top center, score top right (small, 7 digits, leading zeros dimmed). Small labels sit in the side margins ("×2.7" on the left, "-13" on the right; what they mean is not visible). The physical keyboard shows about 4 octaves, C to B (28 white, 20 black, ~48 keys), though the camera may crop the ends.

**Notes.** Short notes are flat rounded bars, white or pale pink, each spanning about 2–3 strip keys. Long notes are wide translucent ribbons, teal or red, that curve along the arches. They end in an outlined cap. In the frames I checked, teal long notes were on the left and red on the right; I could not confirm a hand rule.

**Feedback.** On a hit you get a gold or white starburst on the bar, a soft pastel light pillar rising above it, and pink-outlined "JUST" text floating just above. The strip keys under the note light teal. The physical keys glow amber or teal; I could not work out the rule from the frames. Faint gold rings stay on the bar briefly after a hit. The combo is a large serif number, and every 100 combo a big translucent ghost of the number swells behind it. Rank letters (C ~2:08, B, A ~2:24, S ~2:34) pop mid-screen in laurels. Corner ornaments appear with the C.

**Motion.** Notes cross the highway in about 0.4–0.5 s (measured at 10 fps around 0:26). Dense passages read as stacks of short bars, and the perspective packs them near the horizon. Pillars and "JUST" text last about 0.3–0.5 s, so they overlap in fast runs. The background changes once (sepia to blue at ~1:21), and a glow wash marks the song's end.

**Palette.** Muted sepia or deep navy backgrounds, white notes, teal and crimson long notes, gold bursts and ornaments, serif and script type. Elegant music-box and storybook mood rather than neon.

**Sound.**
- Integrated loudness is -20.7 LUFS. During play the momentary loudness stays flat at about -19 to -21 LUFS, 0:24 to 2:39.
- At 0:07–0:12 there are sparse isolated pitched tones while the player touches keys on the title card. That's consistent with the keys making sound, but I can't confirm it.
- The rest at 1:37.5–1:39.6 drops to -33 LUFS and lines up with the empty highway.
- I found no separate hit transient: the spectrograms show stacked piano-like partials and faint beat-spaced broadband lines, and hits can't be told apart from the music.
- Milestones are silent: there's no loudness change at the C or S ranks or at 600 combo. "Full Combo!" plays over near-silence (-33 LUFS) after the music ends.
- Broadband bursts occur at ~2:44.6 (title-card wipe) and ~2:51 (results screen).
- Limits: mono images and numbers only. I can't separate the backing track from the played notes or from game sounds.

**Steal.**
1. A full-width judgment bar with a key strip right under it that lights under the active note: the highway-to-key mapping becomes obvious.
2. Wide notes spanning several keys, with "JUST" floating above the hit spot. On 88 thin lanes, put judgments and bursts above the bar, not inside the lane.
3. One huge centered combo plus a ghost swell every 100. Score small in a corner.
4. Big ambient cues for progress: a background crossfade, rank letters, corner ornaments, an end-of-song glow wash.
5. Gold burst + rising light pillar as the hit effect: readable at a glance and fading fast.

**Avoid.**
1. Notes that span 2–3 keys. A learner on a real piano needs one exact key per note.
2. Arched lanes and a 0.4 s note window. Too fast and too curved to read pitch; use straight lanes and a slower window.
3. Silent judgments. A learner benefits from a hit or miss cue the music doesn't mask.
