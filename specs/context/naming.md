# Naming: Étude, and 100 ideas

## Abstract

**Piano Arcade** is our working name. It describes the arcade half of the product but not the tutor half, which is
what we're actually selling. This doc proposes a final name, gives the reasons, and lists 100 ideas plus five
alternatives so everyone can vote or veto.

My suggestion is **Étude**. An étude is a piece written to teach one skill, which is exactly what the app does: it
hears every note, finds the one thing you're stuck on, and has you practice that. It's clearly music and clearly
piano. It's classy, and it sits in a different category from a typical hackathon name: no portmanteau, no "-ify", no
"AI" tacked on, just a real word with centuries of craft behind it. It also pairs on purpose with our electric,
arcade look: the name carries the promise (a real tutor), and the look carries the feeling (one more try).

Variants, if we want them, are **Étude
Op. 1** and **Arcade Étude**. The top five alternatives are Rubato, Page Turner, Piano Arc, Da Capo and Duet. Please
vote or veto by morning, so the video's title card can be made.

## My suggestion: Étude

**What the word means.** An étude (French for "study") is a piece written to practice one skill, like Chopin's études.
Each one teaches a single hard thing, and you play it until that thing is yours.

**Why it fits the product.** The app hears every note, finds the bars you trip on, and gives you exactly that to
practice: adaptive chunks narrowing onto your weak bars, a theory card when you need it, one clear next step. That is
an étude, made for you, every take.

**Why I like it.** It's formal and classy, with "the little marks": the accent on the É. It sits in deliberate
contrast to the synthwave-arcade look, and that contrast is the point.

**Why it works as a hackathon name.**
- **It breaks the genre.** Hackathon names follow a pattern: portmanteaus, "-ify" and "-ly" endings, "AI" or "GPT"
  stuck on, puns. A judge scrolling a gallery of them has seen it all. Étude is a real word with centuries of craft
  behind it. It doesn't shout, and that's why it's noticed: it stands out by restraint, not volume.
- **It signals taste before anyone sees the product.** Products that use plain, real words read as made by people who
  know exactly what they're making. Étude says "a team with taste built this" in one word, and the Duo look and the
  sharp corners keep that promise on screen.
- **It's an insider's word that outsiders still feel.** Pianists recognize it at once, so a judge who plays thinks
  "these people know the craft." A judge who doesn't play still hears something classical and serious. Both readings
  help.
- **It's confident.** The name doesn't explain the product, because it doesn't need to. The video does that, and the
  restraint reads as confidence.
- **The tension is memorable.** A classical name on an electric, arcade body is the whole thesis in one pairing: the
  way pianists have always gotten better, built with this year's technology. That's also a clean answer to "Fresh off
  the frontier": an old form, a new machine.

**The honest counterargument.** Classy can read cold: a judge might expect a serious, quiet app and be surprised by
the juice. That surprise works in our favor only if the cold open lands. Some people also won't know how to say it
("ay-tood"), so say it out loud once in the video.

**Why Andres would like it, from his own arguments** (`specs/context/dialogues/2026-10-25-what-are-we-building.md`):
- **"The dream we sell is learn to play piano. That calls for something more formal."** We gave the *look* to the
  arcade, the electric Duo HUD. Étude gives the *name* to the tutor. Positioning is his layer and the look is mine;
  the name is where his layer shows first.
- **"Resolve feeling lost" and "one prepared next action"** (`PRODUCT_PHILOSOPHY.md`): an étude is one clear task, not a
  menu. It's the tutor's whole method in one word.
- **"Believe in capability and encourage stretch":** études exist to stretch a player toward independence, not to
  coddle. That's his principle 4 exactly.
- **The declarative side carries the impact:** études are the classical tradition of *structured* learning. The name
  says "this is real teaching" before anyone sees a combo.

**The honest risks.**
- **The accent:** people will type "Etude". Use "Étude" on screen, and "etude" in URLs and handles.
- **Distinct variants:**
  - **Étude Op. 1:** "our first work", classy, and the Op. number makes it ownable;
  - **Étude for Two Hands:** ties to the Duo brand;
  - **Arcade Étude:** says both halves out loud.

## Top 5 alternatives
1. **Rubato:** tempo that bends to the player. Musical and distinctive; it's what flow mode does, and it's a word
   pianists love.
2. **Page Turner:** the person who sits beside a pianist and keeps them on track. A perfect tutor metaphor, warm, and
   a pun ("a page-turner").
3. **Piano Arc:** keeps the working name's DNA. An electric arc, a learning arc, and the first three letters of arcade.
4. **Da Capo:** "from the top". Play it again, a little better: the brand's "one more time", in a formal word.
5. **Duet:** two hands, or you and your tutor. It matches the Duo look.
